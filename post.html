Why a dedicated reader page

The rest of the site is dense on purpose — boxy grids, small type, a lot packed into one screen. That works for scanning a homepage, but it's a bad way to actually read two thousand words about a Frida hook or a kernel bug. So posts get their own quieter layout: one column, generous line spacing, and nothing competing for attention.

The controls above

A few small things make longer posts easier to sit through:

  • Text size — bump it up on a phone, or down if you just prefer denser text. It's remembered the next time you open a post.
  • Width — narrow for a tight, book-like column; wide if you'd rather use more of the screen on a big monitor.
  • Focus mode — hides the sidebar entirely so the article is the only thing on screen.
  • Progress — the thin bar under the top bar fills in as you scroll, and the estimated read time updates live based on actual word count.
A blog post should be easy to start and hard to put down by accident — not because of tricks, just because nothing gets in the way.
